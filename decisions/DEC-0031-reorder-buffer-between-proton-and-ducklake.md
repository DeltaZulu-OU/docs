# DEC-0031 — A short event-time reorder buffer sits between Proton and DuckLake

## Context

Proton writes two streams into DuckLake: Gold records and the alerts that cite
them (`DEC-0027`, `DEC-0030`). Written directly, records land in arrival order,
which produces two problems.

An alert can land citing Gold rows that are not yet persisted, so evidence fails
to resolve for an unbounded interval. And DuckLake's Gold accumulates in arrival
order rather than time order, which defeats time-range pruning — the access
pattern of essentially every hunting query.

## Decision

A **short-window buffer sits between Proton and DuckLake**, sorting records by
the extracted event time that already governs Gold (`DEC-0029`).

Both Gold and alerts flow through it. Sorting only one stream would provide no
ordering relationship between them, which is half the reason it exists.

The buffer is **not a replay source of truth**. It may be lightweight, because
records lost in flight are regenerated by replaying the Silver buffer through
Proton. That cheapness is purchased by the replay path.

## Consequences

- Evidence resolution is bounded rather than open-ended: an alert can only
  outrun its Gold rows by the window.
- DuckLake Gold becomes time-clustered, so time-range predicates prune. This
  would justify the buffer on its own, independent of the ordering argument.
- **The latency lands on hunt freshness, not detection.** Detection runs on
  Proton's live Gold and is untouched by the window. Retrospective data paying a
  retrospective delay is the right place for the cost.
- **The buffer's cheapness is conditional.** It may be non-durable only while
  replay is wired and the DuckLake write is idempotent on the stable event
  identifier. If replay is ever weakened, this buffer silently becomes a
  durability requirement, and nothing about the buffer itself will signal that.
- **Late arrivals past the window must be appended out of order.** Dropping them
  would be loss, which `DEC-0016` does not permit silently. DuckLake Gold is
  therefore *mostly* sorted, not sorted, and no reader may assume strict
  ordering.
- A crash between the Gold write and the alert write still leaves a dangling
  reference. The buffer bounds the ordinary case; it does not eliminate the
  failure case.
- Ties need a defined precedence. An alert sharing its citing event's timestamp
  must not sort ahead of it — either the alert carries its detection time, which
  is strictly at or after the event time, or the buffer applies an explicit
  Gold-before-alert precedence at equal timestamps.

## Alternatives rejected

| Alternative | Why not | Constraint it failed |
|---|---|---|
| Write directly from Proton to DuckLake | Leaves evidence resolution unbounded and Gold clustered by arrival, defeating the pruning key for every hunting query | — |
| Sort inside DuckLake after the fact | Rewrites already-written data, which conflicts with append-only storage and pays the cost repeatedly instead of once | — |
| Make the buffer durable and replayable | Duplicates the Silver buffer's job. Two replay sources of truth means deciding which one is authoritative after a divergence | — |
| Buffer Gold only, write alerts directly | Removes the ordering relationship between an alert and the rows it cites, which is the correctness half of the rationale | — |
| A long window instead of a short one | Trades hunt freshness for an ordering guarantee already adequate at short windows, and enlarges the non-durable in-flight set | — |

## Revisit trigger

Reopen if late arrivals past the window become common enough that DuckLake Gold's
clustering stops being useful — at which point the question is why events are
arriving that late, not whether the window should grow. Also reopen if replay is
weakened or removed, since this Decision depends on it for the buffer to remain
non-durable.
